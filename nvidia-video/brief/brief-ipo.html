01 / 07 · What an IPO isANT
The basics

Private company, public shares.

An initial public offering (IPO) is the first time a private company sells its shares to the public. After that, anyone can buy and sell them on a stock exchange, any trading day.

How an IPO works

1
The company files a prospectus (Form S-1) with the SEC: its business, finances and risks.
2
Bankers hold a "roadshow", presenting the company to large investors to test demand.
3
A price range and share count are published in an amended filing.
4
The final price is set the evening before trading; the next morning the stock opens.

Words you'll hear

  • Valuation: what the whole company is worth at the IPO price.
  • Amount raised: cash from the shares sold in the offering.
  • Opening price: the first traded price; it can differ a lot from the IPO price.
  • Lock-up: a period when insiders can't sell. Its expiry can add supply to the market.
  • Anchor investor: a large investor that commits early, signalling demand.
Who gets shares at the IPO price?

Mostly institutions through the underwriting banks. Most retail investors buy once trading starts, at the market price.

Underwriters · reported

Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Citi are reported to be working on the deal.

Share structure · reported

Reports say Anthropic is considering super-voting shares for its founders, a common structure in tech IPOs.

Why it could help NVIDIA

  • Multi-year commitments give visibility on demand well beyond the next quarter.
  • A successful mega-IPO funds Anthropic's chip purchases with public money.
  • The filing is a public, audited picture of how much AI labs spend on compute.

Why it's not a guarantee

  • Commitments are not chip revenue booked by NVIDIA; terms and timing can change.
  • Anthropic deliberately buys from Google, Amazon and AMD too.
  • If the IPO disappoints, the whole AI trade can wobble, NVIDIA included.
Bottom line

A strong demand signal for NVIDIA's chips, and a reason the next NVIDIA earnings report (17 Nov) and the Anthropic debut will be read together.

How the price gets set

1
Bankers test demand on the roadshow.
2
A price range is published in an amended filing.
3
If demand is strong, the range can be raised.
4
The final price is set the night before trading.

Why early estimates disagree

Blogs and brokers have published "implied" prices from footnotes in the filing. They range from roughly $70 to $100 per share, largely because of a 10-for-1 share split and different valuation assumptions. None of them is an offering term.

What to do with estimates

Treat them as guesses. Only the price range in the amended filing, and the final price the night before, count. We will update this brief when they are published.

06 / 07 · What to watchANT
What to watch

Three things to watch.

1 · The official price range

Expected in an amended filing before the roadshow ends. It sets the valuation and how much is raised.

2 · The first day of trading

The opening price can differ a lot from the IPO price. SpaceX opened at $150 against a $135 IPO price and closed up 19%. Early trading is often volatile in both directions.

3 · Demand from big investors

Oversubscribed books often lift the range. Reports of anchor investors (NVIDIA is said to be weighing ~$10B) are one signal.
